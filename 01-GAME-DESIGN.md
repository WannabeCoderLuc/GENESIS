# Game Design Specification

## Pillars

1. **Scale is progression.** Each promotion changes decisions, controls, presentation, and automation.
2. **Creation has continuity.** The first star remains addressable forever, even when it becomes one point inside a cosmic web.
3. **Active mastery beats clicking.** Intervention improves outcomes; passive systems preserve progress without demanding constant attention.
4. **Safe ownership, optional risk.** Home universes cannot be griefed. Competitive loss exists only in explicitly entered modes with bounded stakes.
5. **Scientific texture, readable choices.** Systems are inspired by astronomy but explain consequences in plain language.
6. **Spectacle confirms meaning.** Every major formation, stabilization, discovery, merger, and promotion has audiovisual payoff.

## Player personas

- **Builder:** seeks a beautiful, coherent universe and visitor appreciation.
- **Optimizer:** tunes energy, stability, automation, and ranked efficiency.
- **Explorer:** hunts anomalies and Archivarium entries.
- **Social host:** curates tours, co-op events, and collaborative projects.
- **Competitor:** plays equal-start challenges and the opt-in Interstitial Void.
- **Long-horizon collector:** returns for epochs, rare laws, catalog completion, and history.

No persona should be forced into destructive PvP or purchases to complete core progression.

## Nested loops

### 5–30 seconds: observe and intervene

Scan, focus, adjust matter or orbit, route a resource, answer a warning, place a module, capture a discovery, or inspect a simulation consequence. The UI always offers a meaningful action but never punishes a player for pausing to watch.

### 2–10 minutes: solve a local objective

Stabilize a star, form a planet, optimize resonance, complete a scan, tune a swarm, resolve an event, or improve a civilization policy. A compact objective ends with visible transformation and an intelligible result card.

### 20–60 minutes: build a system

Complete a stellar neighborhood, unlock automation, finish a megastructure stage, host a visit, or enter a Cosmic Challenge.

### Days: graduate scale

Promote from system management to galactic sculpting, complete a merger, build a cluster network, or prepare a new epoch. Offline simulation advances capped, deterministic summaries; it never causes irreversible catastrophe.

### Months and beyond: identity and mastery

Collect rare phenomena, specialize Cosmic Index categories, refine multiple archived epochs, build visit-worthy showcases, and participate in non-destructive seasons.

## Progression ladder

| Tier | Player verbs | New system | Automation earned |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Star | condense, heat, balance, ignite | stellar variables | safe baseline stabilization |
| Planetary System | accrete, orbit, resonate, terraform | bodies and habitability | accretion templates |
| Living Worlds | nurture, observe, catalog | biosphere statistics | ecosystem monitoring |
| Civilizations | influence, trade, protect, study | autonomous societies | policy and logistics agents |
| Stellar Neighborhood | seed, connect, specialize | interstellar network | star/system governors |
| Stellar Engineering | construct, route, harvest | megastructures | construction queues |
| Galaxy | sculpt, regulate, merge | galactic morphology | regional governors |
| Cluster | coordinate, migrate, arbitrate | intergalactic systems | cluster policies |
| Cosmic Web | shape, balance, discover | filaments and voids | cosmological planners |
| New Epoch | archive, choose laws, restart differently | altered rules | legacy knowledge |

Promotion requires a demonstrated mastery goal, not only a currency total. Old interactions remain inspectable, but routine execution becomes automatable.

## Resources

Use a small, legible family with different roles:

- **Primordial Matter:** formation mass; conserved enough to make allocation meaningful.
- **Stellar Influence:** local intervention budget generated by stable stars.
- **Knowledge:** earned through first discoveries, scanning, experiments, and visitors; unlocks methods.
- **Exotic Matter:** rare, bounded, primarily from anomalies and the optional Void.
- **Civilization Capacity:** aggregate ability of societies to build and coordinate.
- **Epoch Insight:** non-purchasable legacy currency earned by completing genuinely different cosmic objectives.

Avoid dozens of currencies. Any proposed resource must enable a distinct decision, have at least two sources and sinks, and remain explainable in one sentence.

## Stellar and orbital design

Stars expose mass, composition/metallicity, temperature, rotation, stability, and fuel balance. The simulation maps them to classes, output, habitable bands, event chances, lifetime, and end state. Planets expose mass, composition, atmosphere, temperature, water state, radiation, orbital parameters, moons, rings, and biosphere potential.

Interactions use constrained handles and previews. Before committing an orbital change, show energy cost, predicted stability, collision risk, resonance, and habitability delta. Destructive outcomes require confirmation and offer a sandbox preview.

## Life and civilizations

Life is statistical, not millions of agents. A biosphere state machine advances through chemistry, microbial, complex, intelligent, and spacefaring thresholds. Civilizations have archetype weights, ethics-neutral behavioral descriptors, technology, stability, population index, energy class, home region, relationships, and projects.

Player influence changes pressures rather than issuing every unit command. Civilizations can surprise the player, but major irreversible outcomes use warnings and intervention windows. Extinctions become history, not routine punishment.

## Events

Event families include stellar instability, supernova preparation, magnetar flares, asteroid chains, rogue objects, biosphere transitions, civilization conflicts, machine awakening, trade booms, anomaly openings, galactic mergers, black-hole activity, and cosmological phase changes.

Each event contains:

- deterministic trigger and seed;
- warning and readable stakes;
- at least two valid approaches;
- passive resolution that is not catastrophic while offline;
- outcome summary and Archivarium hooks;
- cooldown and repetition controls.

## Social and competitive systems

The Cosmic Observatory is the public hub and discovery board. Players may visit private, friends-only, public, or collaborative universes. Hosts grant capability-scoped permissions; visitors default to read-only exploration. Helpful actions are proposals or bounded co-op interactions committed by the host server.

Cosmic Challenges give all entrants identical seeds and budgets. Rankings record efficiency, stability, biodiversity, beauty-vote eligibility, or discovery—not a single power score. The Interstitial Void is an opt-in expedition place where only explicitly deployed resources are at risk. Home state is never attackable.

## Archivarium and goals

Every discovery is a typed record with rarity, first-seen epoch, object address, snapshot metadata, and optional cosmetic exhibit. Collections create guided goals at all scales. Secret entries are inferred through hints rather than opaque random drops. Duplicate discoveries provide modest research value, not frustration.

## Failure, recovery, and pacing

- Ordinary mistakes create repair gameplay and interesting history.
- Catastrophes use forecasts and safeguards; offline time cannot erase months of work.
- New players reach ignition in 60–90 seconds and a first stable planet in the first session.
- Sessions end at natural save points with a visible next objective.
- Catch-up systems restore knowledge and automation, not leaderboard parity bought with money.

## Balance principles

- Prefer diminishing returns, capacity constraints, and tradeoffs to hard time walls.
- Active play improves quality or speed by a bounded amount; idling never becomes optimal forever.
- No system may require continuous attendance longer than 20 minutes without a pause/checkpoint.
- Randomness selects situations; player decisions determine most outcomes.
- All competitive modes use normalized inputs or clear divisions.

## Initial vertical-slice content

One star class continuum, six planet archetypes, orbital placement/resonance, three anomaly types, one simple biosphere track, one Observatory visit flow, a three-player equal-seed challenge, twelve Archivarium entries, photo mode, and the first scale transition. Exclude civilizations, galaxy mergers, and the Void until the foundation proves fun and performant.

## Studio validation and reusable design skills

Use the `Roblox_Studio` MCP server during design validation to run the actual onboarding, scale transition, visit, and equal-seed challenge flows; inspect UI and scene state; and capture playtest evidence instead of approving mechanics from prose alone. An agent must first verify the Studio connection, target `studio_id`, and relevant built-in skill availability. Useful skills may include `rbx-debug` for runtime state and, when performance affects the feel of a mechanic, `rbx-perf-profiling` or `rbx-scene-analysis`; treat those names as capabilities to discover, not guaranteed tools.

Create a project custom skill when a design test has a stable setup, sequence, metrics, and pass/fail rule that will recur across milestones—for example `universe-first-session-review` or `universe-scale-transition-review`. Store the skill at project scope, keep subjective design approval outside automated claims, and follow the naming and progressive-disclosure guidance in `roblox_skills_guide.md`. One-off balance explorations stay as work packets rather than skills.
